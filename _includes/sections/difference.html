What I carry that most architects don’t

Most architects specialise in either architecture, product, or experience. My path ran through all three — and through running a business that had to survive its own delivery decisions.

  • Founder and operator — built a 50-person digital services practice over 11 years and led it through acquisition
  • Commercial instinct — business cases, build-vs-buy, vendor negotiation, and delivery accountability for enterprise accounts
  • Design origin — Creative Director for five years before architecture; award-winning UX still in my own hands
  • Both operating modes — startup velocity and enterprise governance, without mistaking one for the other
  • Still builds — a full-stack engineering background (MERN, TypeScript) behind the architecture diagrams

That combination is why I can diagnose systemic problems quickly: I have sat on every side of the table where the problem was created. The result is architecture that is technically sound, commercially defensible, and experientially coherent.

Principles I design by:

Architect information, not just interfaces
Reduce friction before adding features
Design for cognition, not aesthetics
Build reusable systems, not isolated screens
Align user journeys with measurable business outcomes

A technically scalable system that fails experientially is still broken.
